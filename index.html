NanoOptic privacy policy

Last updated: 27 November 2026

NanoOptic is a camera app made by NanoNexus. It has no account, no ads and no servers of its own, and it does not sell your data. Your photos, videos and audio are saved on your phone. They leave it only when you share them, upload them to your own server, copy them to a drive or folder you choose, or view the camera through the web remote you switch on. This policy says what the app handles, including the technical data one Google library sends.

Sharing and your location

When you share a photo, a DNG or a video from the app's gallery, "Strip location" (on by default) removes the location from the copy that is shared; the file on your phone is not changed. If the app cannot remove it or cannot check that it is gone (for example from some HEIC photos), it asks you before sharing. When you share edit settings (XMP) together with a DNG, the DNG goes without its location, or is left out. A stamp that prints the coordinates on a picture or a time-lapse is part of the image, so removing the location does not remove it. Uploading to your server and copying to a drive or watched folder keep the files as they are, including their location.

Portrait mode, face detection and Google ML Kit

Portrait mode uses Google's ML Kit library to find the person in the picture. Face detection, also from ML Kit, drives the scene suggestions, the composition hints and the best-shot choice for bursts in the gallery. The models are inside the app and the picture is processed on your phone. It is not sent to Google, and the app does not store or send face data: the boxes and probabilities it finds are used for the moment and discarded. ML Kit itself sends Google technical data about the library: information about the device (such as maker, model and Android version), the app's package name and version, an identifier for this installation that is not meant to identify you or your phone, performance figures, the settings and size of what was processed, event types and error codes. Google uses it for diagnostics and usage analytics. Google says this data is sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) and that ML Kit does not pass it to third parties: developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure. NanoOptic does not add anything to that data, and the app has no switch to turn it off.

"Blur faces" in the photo editor uses the face detector built into Android, on the phone.

Voice commands and teleprompter

Voice commands and the teleprompter's "follow my voice" are off by default. When you turn one on, NanoOptic uses the speech recognition service built into Android. NanoOptic asks it to work on the phone when it can, but depending on your phone the service (usually Google's) may send the audio to its provider to turn it into text. NanoOptic does not record, store or send that audio itself, and the provider's own terms apply to it. "Follow my voice" is switched off while a video is recording.

Network use

NanoOptic works without a connection. It has two optional network features, both off until you use them. The first is the web remote: a page you can open from another device on your own Wi-Fi (or the NanoOptic Studio app on a Windows computer) to see the viewfinder and control the camera, including starting and stopping a recording and comparing clocks when you use several phones together. It is off by default, needs an eight-digit PIN that changes each time it starts, and is encrypted (HTTPS with a self-signed certificate) unless you turn encryption off. After repeated wrong PINs from one device the phone refuses that device for a while. The remote listens on the phone's network address and is meant for your own Wi-Fi, but the PIN is its only protection: anyone who can reach that address (on a shared or public Wi-Fi, for example) can try to guess it. Use the web remote only on a network you trust and switch it off when you are done.

The second network feature is the optional "Upload to my server". It does nothing until you set it up. It sends only the photos, videos and DNG files you select in the gallery, and only when you tap Upload, to the server address you typed (for example your own NAS). NanoNexus and other companies receive nothing, and nothing is uploaded automatically or in the background. Uploads use HTTPS with the phone's normal certificate checks. Plain HTTP works only if you switch on "Allow plain HTTP on my home network", and only for home-network addresses; on plain HTTP other people on that network can read what you send, including the password. The app does not follow redirects to another address. The server address, user name and folder are kept on the phone, and the password is kept only in encrypted form with a key in the phone's secure hardware. None of these are included in Android's backup. "Forget the server and the password" erases them.

External drive

If you choose a folder on a USB drive or SD card, a finished video or photo (with its DNG file, if you shoot RAW) is copied there after it is saved (the "watched folder" option copies each new photo, its DNG file and its metadata file the same way to a folder you pick), under a temporary name ending in ".part" until the copy has been checked against the original, and the original on the phone is deleted only if you tap "Delete original from the phone". NanoOptic gets access only to the folder you chose, and you can remove that access in the app or in Android.

Send feedback

"Send feedback" prepares a text with your note and, if you leave them ticked, the device report, the latest crash log and local performance figures. You see the exact text before it goes, and it leaves the phone only when you send it from your own e-mail or sharing app. If you e-mail it to us we receive your e-mail address and that text. We use them only to answer you and to fix problems, keep them only as long as that takes, and delete them when you ask at nanonexuslabs@gmail.com.

Crash information

If the app crashes, the details are saved on the phone only. They leave it only if you export the device report or use "Send feedback" and send the text yourself. The performance numbers the app keeps (such as camera open time and shutter-to-saved time) stay on the phone, are off the screen unless you switch the overlay on, and are never sent by the app.

Signed captures

If you turn on "Sign captures", NanoOptic signs each file with a key that lives in your phone's secure hardware and writes a small record next to the file. The key never leaves the phone. The record lets someone with the file check that it has not changed since it was captured.

Android backup

If backup is switched on in Android, the system may copy the app's settings and private files to your Google account, or to a new phone: settings, ratings, tags and notes, the photo information you typed, the watermark logo, looks, edit settings, dark frames and the word-search index. It does not include the upload server settings or its password, the external-drive folder choice, the app's signed-capture record, crash logs or temporary files. Your photos and videos are not part of this app backup (a photo service you use may back them up under its own terms). Android limits the size of an app's backup and may skip it if it grows beyond the limit. You manage and delete backups in your Android and Google account settings.

How your data is protected

Settings and private files are kept in the app's private storage, which Android keeps apart from other apps. The upload password is encrypted with a key held by the phone's secure hardware. Uploads use HTTPS unless you allow plain HTTP on your home network, and the web remote uses HTTPS unless you turn encryption off.

Keeping and deleting data

NanoNexus runs no server that collects your data. What we receive is what you send us yourself, such as a feedback e-mail (see "Send feedback"). Data on your phone stays until you delete it. Uninstalling the app removes its settings and private files; your photos, videos, audio and the files in Documents/NanoOptic stay until you delete them. Copies you shared, uploaded or copied elsewhere must be deleted there, and an Android backup in your Google account is removed through your account settings. Data that ML Kit sends to Google is handled under Google's terms.

Children

NanoOptic is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect their data.

Your choices

  • Switch off location, the web remote or signed captures in the app's settings at any time, and use "Forget the server and the password" to erase the upload settings.
  • Deny or remove the camera, microphone or location permission in Android settings.
  • Switch off "Geotag" to keep location out of new files, and leave "Strip location" on when sharing.
  • Delete your photos and videos like any other files. Uninstalling the app removes its settings.
